# AtoZ — Current Model vs. Marketplace Model

> Side-by-side comparison of income potential, capital requirements, growth dynamics, and exit value between the **current vertically-integrated AtoZ business** and the **proposed managed marketplace**.
>
> Numbers for the current model are *illustrative averages* for a London bespoke installer of AtoZ's scale; replace with AtoZ's actual figures before any decision. Marketplace numbers come from `TURNOVER_ANALYSIS.md`.

---

## 1. One-page summary

| | **Current AtoZ** (vertically integrated) | **Marketplace AtoZ** (managed broker) |
|---|---|---|
| What you sell | Bespoke windows / doors, designed + supplied + installed by AtoZ | Same products, fulfilled by panel of suppliers + panel of installers |
| Who carries the risk | AtoZ (full liability chain) | Shared via back-to-back agreements + escrow |
| Capacity ceiling | Your own crews (~10–15 jobs/month) | Panel capacity (~30+ jobs/month, scalable) |
| Capital tied up | High — vans, tools, stock, premises, payroll | Low — software, marketing, lean ops |
| Cash cycle | Negative (you pay suppliers + payroll before customer pays) | Positive (escrow + back-to-back) |
| Gross margin | 25–35% retained on the job | 20–25% blended after splits |
| **Realistic Y1 turnover** | **£1.0–1.8m** (already running) | **£0.85m–£2.7m** (depending on ramp) |
| **Realistic Y3 turnover** | **£1.5–2.5m** (linear, capacity-limited) | **£3m–£10m** (panel scales, B2B unlocks) |
| Growth shape | Linear, capital-intensive | Sub-linear capital → step-function with B2B |
| Owner role | On the tools, in every job | Panel + BD + escalations |
| Exit value (multiple) | ~2–3× EBITDA (trade buyer) | ~4–8× EBITDA (tech-enabled marketplace) |

| Installer panel cost | — | £2,200 |

| Marketing CAC (allocated) | £400 | £600 |

| Line | Monthly | Annual |

| Jobs | 10 | 120 |
| AOV | £12,000 | — |
| Turnover | £120,000 | **£1.44m** |

Above that you need a second showroom, more vans, more crews — i.e. another small business bolted onto the first.

## 4. Capital requirements

| | Current AtoZ | Marketplace |
|---|---|---|
| Vans (3–4 vehicles) | £80–120k | £0 — installer panel uses theirs |
| Tools, plant, scaffold | £20–40k | £0 — installer panel |
| Stock / showroom samples | £30–80k | £5–15k (samples only) |
| Premises bond / fit-out | £20–50k | £0 (or keep current AtoZ HQ) |
| Working capital float | £60–100k | £60–90k |
| Software / platform build | £0–5k (basic CRM) | £30–60k (configurator, portals, escrow integration) |
| **Total tied-up capital** | **£210–395k** | **£95–165k** |

Marketplace ties up roughly **half** the capital of the current model and the largest single cost (software) is a one-off, not a recurring one.

---

## 5. Cash-cycle comparison

### Current AtoZ
1. Customer pays 30% deposit → bank
2. AtoZ pays supplier 50–100% upfront → cash out
3. AtoZ pays fitters weekly (PAYE) → cash out
4. Customer pays balance on completion → bank
5. **Net:** AtoZ funds ~30–60 days of working capital per job

### Marketplace
1. Customer pays 30% into **escrow** (not AtoZ)
2. Manufacturer paid by escrow on dispatch (not by AtoZ)
3. Installer paid by escrow on sign-off (not by AtoZ)
4. AtoZ's margin released last
5. **Net:** AtoZ funds essentially zero per job

This single difference is why the marketplace can scale to £5m+ on a working-capital cushion that wouldn't get the current model past £2m.

## 6. Risk profile

| Risk | Current AtoZ | Marketplace |
|---|---|---|
| Bad install on a £15k bifold | Direct loss + reputation hit. AtoZ pays remake. | Installer panel agreement assigns liability. AtoZ supervises. |
| Manufacturer late delivery | AtoZ wears the customer's anger; nothing to switch to | Switch to alternate panel manufacturer |
| Slow month (no jobs) | Payroll still due (£20–25k/month) → losses | Variable cost base — quiet month is cheap |
| Founder ill / on holiday | Business stalls | Operations runs on the platform + ops lead |
| Customer disputes payment | AtoZ chases, often loses balance | Escrow holds money, deemed acceptance after 14 days |
| Insurance claim on the IBG | Direct hit | Insurer pays; subrogation against installer |

The marketplace **doesn't remove risk — it redistributes it** across panel members and insurance. Properly structured, AtoZ's tail risk is much lower.

## 7. Growth dynamics

### Current AtoZ
Growth is **linear and front-loaded with capital**:
- +1 fitter team = +£300k turnover, but +£90k payroll, +£35k van/tools, 3-month lead time
- More crews = more management overhead on the founder
- Each marginal job earns less because complexity and supervision cost compound

### Marketplace
Growth is **sub-linear in cost**:
- +1 panel installer = +£300k+ capacity at £0 capex (just contract)
- +1 panel manufacturer = new tier opens, no capex
- Configurator scales to 10× volume at near-zero marginal cost
- B2B accounts are step-functions (£300k–£1m each)

## 8. Founder / owner time

| | Current AtoZ | Marketplace |
|---|---|---|
| Site days / week | 3–5 | 0–1 |
| Customer calls / week | 15–25 | 2–5 (only escalations) |
| New-business calls | Squeeze in evenings | Core part of the role |
| Holiday-without-stress | Hard | Possible from Y2 |
| Time spent on £-multiplying activities (BD, partnerships, brand) | <10% | 60%+ |

In the current model the founder *is* the operation. In the marketplace the founder *runs* the operation — that's the difference between owning a job and owning a business.

## 9. Exit value

| | Current AtoZ | Marketplace |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer profile | Local trade buyer / competitor consolidator | Trade buyer **or** PE / strategic / tech buyer |
| Multiple of EBITDA | 2–3× | 4–8× (tech-enabled, recurring B2B revenue) |
| Y3 EBITDA (base) | ~£100–150k → **value £200–450k** | ~£500k → **value £2.0–4.0m** |
| Y3 EBITDA (stretch) | n/a (capacity-bound) | ~£1.1m → **value £4.4–8.8m** |
| Sellability | Owner-dependent → harder | Process-dependent → easier |

**The exit-value gap is where the marketplace really pays off.** Same founder, same hours, same ten-year window — but the asset built is 5–20× more valuable when sold.

## 10. What the current model is *better* at

Be honest about what AtoZ would be giving up:

- **Quality control** — when your crew installs, you set the standard. Panel installers vary; you have to enforce.
- **Margin per job** — at the unit level, in-house can extract a couple of % more if you're efficient.
- **Brand intimacy** — high-touch craftsmanship is part of why customers choose AtoZ today. Marketplace is more "platform" feeling. Branding has to work harder.
- **Speed to start** — current model is already running. Marketplace needs setup, panel onboarding, software.

The right decision is rarely "kill the old, build the new". It's **run them in parallel and let the marketplace eat into the legacy mix on its own terms**.

---

## 11. Recommended hybrid path

Don't shut down the current operation. Migrate it.

| Phase | Months | What's running |
|---|---|---|
| **Phase 0** | 0–3 | Current AtoZ runs as today. Marketplace contracts + escrow + first panel signed in background. |
| **Phase 1** | 3–9 | First 10 marketplace jobs run in parallel with current jobs. Crew capacity stays flat. New jobs route to panel. |
| **Phase 2** | 9–18 | Marketplace overtakes legacy in new-job count. Old fitter team either becomes the lead panel installer or transitions to retainer. |
| **Phase 3** | 18+ | AtoZ is the brand and platform. Old crew is one of several on the panel. Founder runs BD + partnerships. |

This avoids the "burn the boats" risk and means revenue never dips during transition.

---

## 12. Verdict

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Does the marketplace earn dramatically more per job? | **No** — the unit economics are within ~£100. |
| Does it scale to materially higher revenue? | **Yes** — 3–5× the current ceiling within 3 years, plausibly 6–8× by year 5. |
| Does it tie up less capital and risk? | **Yes** — roughly half the capital, far better cash cycle, distributed liability. |
| Does it free the founder? | **Yes** — gradually, fully by Y2. |
| Is it worth more as an asset? | **Yes** — 2–3× the multiple on a higher EBITDA base. |
| Is it riskier in the short term? | **Yes** — Phase 0–1 has setup, legal, panel-onboarding risk. |
| Should it replace AtoZ overnight? | **No** — run in parallel; let it earn the right to dominate the mix. |

**Bottom line:** the current AtoZ model is a healthy small-to-mid-sized owner-operator business with a ceiling around £1.5–2m turnover and ~£100k owner profit. The marketplace model has a ceiling around £8–12m turnover and £1m+ profit, with a saleable asset at the end worth several million rather than several hundred thousand. The two earn similar money in Year 1; everything after that diverges sharply in the marketplace's favour.
