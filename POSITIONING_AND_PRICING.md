# AtoZ Marketplace — Why Clients Buy & Commission Strategy

> Two questions answered:
>
> 1. **Why will clients buy from AtoZ?** What demand drivers actually move people in the UK windows/doors market, and how the marketplace satisfies them better than the alternatives.
> 2. **What % commission should AtoZ charge?** Including how to position the "factory direct" angle without falling foul of the ASA or breaking unit economics.

## 1. The demand-side question — what makes a UK customer buy?

### 1.1 The five buying alternatives a customer is choosing between

| Option | Typical price (3-pane bifold, fitted) | Friction |
|---|---|---|
| National installer (Anglian / Everest / Safestyle) | £10–14k | High-pressure sales, slow quotes, multiple visits |
| Local independent installer | £7–10k | Inconsistent quality, smaller guarantee, harder to compare |
| Architect-specified premium installer | £12–18k | Premium price, premium service, only via specifier |
| Buy direct from Polish factory + find own fitter | £4.5–7k all-in | Language, customs, snagging at distance, no IBG |
| Lead-gen / comparison site (Quotatis etc.) | Variable | They sell the lead — customer fields multiple sales calls |

**AtoZ marketplace target price for the same job: £6.5–8k fitted.** That puts AtoZ **25–40% below the national installers** while staying ~30% above the cheapest direct-import-and-DIY-fit route.

### 1.2 What UK research actually says drives the decision

Industry data and consumer research point to seven decision drivers, in order:

| Rank | Driver | What customers say | How AtoZ satisfies it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Trust + accountability** | "If something goes wrong, who do I call?" | One legal entity (AtoZ), CERTASS-registered, IBG, Trustpilot reviews |
| 2 | **Energy performance** | "What's the U-value?" — 60% of pros cite this as #1 ask | Tier system makes U-values visible; premium tier = passive-house-grade |
| 3 | **Price vs perceived value** | "Why is the national company twice the local one?" | "European factory pricing, UK installation" — credible discount story |
| 4 | **Insurance-backed guarantee** | Property value uplift requires evidence on resale | HomePro IBG issued at sign-off |
| 5 | **Single point of contact** | Tired of chasing manufacturer + fitter separately | Customer success owner per job; one portal, one phone number |
| 6 | **Speed + transparency** | "Why does it take 5 days to get a quote?" | Indicative quote in <1 hour; firm quote within 5 days of survey |
| 7 | **Aesthetic / bespoke fit** | "Will it match the existing windows?" | Bespoke is the default — every job is to-measure |

Notable: **price alone is not #1.** Trust is. UK consumer behaviour around windows is: *"low prices signal poor installation that will fail sooner"* — this is the well-documented suspicion that makes the cheapest quote often lose.

### 1.3 The competitive insight that opens the door for AtoZ

The big four UK installers (Anglian, Everest, Safestyle, Sehbac) are **all now owned by the same parent** (ASHI Group consolidating the market in 2024). They:

- Are widely seen as overpriced — Anglian uPVC runs **20–40% above equivalent local FENSA installers**, with quote variation of 100%+ between national and local for the same job
- Use high-pressure sales tactics that consumers actively dislike
- Have low Trustpilot scores and recurring "today-only price drop" complaints

**The market gap AtoZ fills:** national-installer-grade trust and guarantee at independent-installer pricing — without the high-pressure sales experience and without the consumer doing the import-from-Poland legwork themselves.

### 1.4 The seven reasons a customer chooses AtoZ over the alternatives

1. **Cheaper than the nationals** — 25–40% saving vs Anglian/Everest equivalent for the same product spec
2. **Safer than DIY-importing** — they don't have to deal with Polish factories, customs, finding a fitter, or "who's responsible if it leaks?"
3. **Faster than getting 3 local quotes** — instant indicative price online, real surveyors, transparent firm quote
4. **Wider product range than any single installer** — entry PVC through to bespoke steel; one platform, every tier
5. **Insurance-backed** — IBG + CERTASS-registered installation, recognised on resale and by mortgage providers
6. **Single throat to choke** — AtoZ owns the contract; if anything goes wrong, one entity is accountable
7. **Real reviews, real brand** — existing Trustpilot/Google reviews, established London identity, not a faceless platform

That's the customer pitch. Each one is independently true and supportable. None of them require the "factory direct" claim to land.

## 2. The "prices direct from the factory" question

### 2.1 What's true, and what's a problem

**True:** AtoZ does buy from factories at trade prices and then add a margin to cover service, install, certification, and profit.

**Problematic:** "Prices direct from the factory" *implies the customer is paying the factory price* — i.e., no mark-up. That's not what's happening. The customer pays factory price + AtoZ margin + install.

If a customer compares AtoZ's £6,500 fitted price for a 3-pane bifold to the £3,800 trade price they can find online from the same Polish manufacturer, the claim collapses.

### 2.2 ASA / CAP rules — the legal exposure

UK marketing must:
- Not materially mislead, including by **omission or implication**
- Not give an "overall impression" likely to deceive consumers
- Be backed by **documentary evidence** for objective claims

A literal "factory prices" claim is the kind of phrase the ASA routinely tests against the overall-impression standard. Possible outcomes if challenged:

- ASA upholds complaint → ad must be withdrawn → reputational hit, indexed by Google
- Trading Standards involvement → fines, mandatory corrective advertising
- Competitor complaint (likely from Anglian/Everest) → forced rewrite

### 2.3 Defensible alternatives that say the same thing without breaking

| Don't say | Do say |
|---|---|
| "Prices direct from the factory" | "Direct from the European factory floor — no national-installer mark-up" |
| "Factory prices" | "Trade pricing, with insured installation" |
| "Cut out the middleman" | "We've cut out the traditional showroom mark-up" |
| "No commission" | "Transparent pricing — see exactly what your money pays for" |
| "Lowest in the UK" | "Save 25–40% vs the leading national installers" (with substantiation) |

The best framing: **"European factory pricing + locally insured installation."** It's true, it's defensible, it's still a strong claim, and it sets up the price story without the ASA exposure.

### 2.4 The optional trust play — pricing transparency

A more powerful version of the "factory direct" message is to actually *show* the breakdown. On the configurator output:

```
Your bespoke 3-pane aluminium bifold

  Factory price (DAKO, Poland)            £3,800
  Delivery + customs + handling             £350
  Survey + installation (CERTASS-reg)     £2,200
  AtoZ service, IBG, 10-yr guarantee        £950   ← visible
  ────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Total fitted                            £7,300
  VAT included
```

This converts "factory direct" from a marketing claim into a **demonstrated fact**. It also frames AtoZ's commission as a service fee for tangible things (IBG, guarantee, customer service) rather than as a hidden mark-up. Customers consistently rate transparent pricing as a top-3 trust signal in home improvement research.

> **Recommendation:** default to a single bundled price for clean UX, but make "see how this is priced" a one-click reveal. The honest claim and the marketing claim become the same thing.

## 3. Commission strategy — what % should AtoZ charge?

### 3.1 What 10% buys you (and why it's too low)

A flat 10% commission can't sustain a marketplace that carries:
- Customer service (~£200–400/job allocated)
- CERTASS umbrella + IBG admin (~£40–80/job)
- Marketing CAC (~£400–800/job)
- Software, escrow, payment processing (~£150–250/job)
- Risk reserve for remakes / disputes (~£100–200/job)
- Profit margin for AtoZ

Total fixed-and-variable per-job cost is **~£1,000–1,800**. On a £10,000 AOV, 10% = £1,000 — you're **break-even or losing money before the founder takes a salary.** The model only works if panel subscription revenue closes the gap, which it doesn't at small scale.

### 3.2 What the market benchmarks suggest

| Marketplace | Take rate | What they do for it |
|---|---|---|
| Airbnb (host-only) | 15.5% | Listing + payments + dispute mediation. **No fulfilment.** |
| Amazon | 8–15% category + flat | Listing + fulfilment available extra. |
| Just Eat | 14–18% | Order routing + payments. **No food, no driver.** |
| Etsy | 6.5% + listing fee | Listing + payments. **No fulfilment.** |
| UK home services lead-gen (Checkatrade etc.) | Subscription only | Lead delivery. **No accountability.** |
| Debesto (windows broker) | 20–35% margin | Sourcing + import + customer service. **No fitting.** |
| Traditional UK installer (Anglian etc.) | 80–150% mark-up | Full service. The number AtoZ is trying to undercut. |

AtoZ does **more** than Airbnb/Etsy (it carries certification + warranty + install supervision) and **less** than a traditional installer (it doesn't own the crew or the factory). Honest take rate sits **between 18% and 25%**, depending on order size and channel.

### 3.3 Recommended commission structure

A **tiered commission**, not a flat number. Lower take on large orders keeps AtoZ competitive on big-ticket B2B. Higher take on small jobs covers fixed per-job overhead.

| Order value (customer pays, ex-VAT) | AtoZ take rate | Why this rate |
|---|---|---|
| Under £3,000 | **25%** | Fixed costs (survey, admin, customer service) eat a fixed-£ amount; needs higher % to cover them |
| £3,000–£10,000 | **22%** | The B2C sweet spot. Sustainable, competitive, supports the value prop |
| £10,000–£30,000 | **18%** | Larger jobs spread fixed costs further |
| £30,000–£100,000 | **15%** | Approaching B2B; price competitiveness matters more |
| £100,000+ (developer / project) | **10–12%** | Volume discount; loses bid otherwise |

**B2B trade accounts** (architects, regular contractors): take an additional **2–3pp off the relevant tier** as a trade discount. Repeat customers, no marketing CAC, predictable cashflow — they earn the discount.

### 3.4 Per-job worked example with new commission

A £7,500 fitted bifold to a B2C customer (sweet-spot tier, 22%):

| Line | £ |
|---|---|
| Customer pays | 7,500 |
| Manufacturer cost (trade price, factory) | 3,800 |
| Logistics, customs, admin | 350 |
| Installer day rate (panel installer net) | 1,700 |
| **Cost of goods + service** | **5,850** |
| **AtoZ gross margin (22%)** | **1,650** |

Of that £1,650 AtoZ retains:
- ~£600 absorbed by CAC, software, escrow, customer service
- ~£100 to risk reserve (remakes, disputes)
- ~£950 contribution to overhead + profit

That last line — £950 retained per job × ~150 jobs/year base case = **~£140k contribution to overhead and profit from the customer-side margin alone**, not counting panel subscriptions and finance referrals.

### 3.5 What the customer sees vs what AtoZ earns

**Customer view (default UX):** one bundled price of £7,500. They compare it favourably to Anglian's £10,500 quote.

**Customer view (transparent UX, on request):**
```
Factory price (Poland)          £3,800
Delivery + handling               £350
Installation (CERTASS-reg)      £1,700
AtoZ service & IBG              £1,650 ← 22%
─────────────────────────────────────
Total fitted, VAT inc.          £7,500
```

**Marketing claim:** "European factory pricing + insured local installation. Save 25–40% on national-installer alternatives."

All of these align. None of them break under scrutiny.

## 4. Why this commission level works for the value prop

### Cheaper than the alternatives — but not the cheapest
At 22% blended, AtoZ is **~30% cheaper than national installers** and ~10–20% more expensive than the cheapest local independent. That's the right position: cheap enough to disrupt, expensive enough to fund proper service and warranty.

### Defensible against direct-from-Poland
A customer comparing AtoZ's £7,500 to the £4,500 they could spend importing themselves and finding their own fitter has to weigh:
- Saving £3,000
- Against: no IBG, no UK accountability, no CERTASS cert (mortgage / sale problem later), language / customs / snagging hassle

Most homeowners pay the £3,000 for the peace of mind. Some don't. The ones who don't were never AtoZ's customer.

### Sustainable for AtoZ
At 22%, every job covers its true cost and contributes meaningfully to overhead. The model breaks even at ~60–80 jobs/year and scales profitably from there.

### Aligned with panel incentives
Panel manufacturers and installers get steady volume; they don't object to AtoZ's margin because they see what AtoZ does (customer service, escrow, snagging) — they just don't want to do it themselves.

## 5. Risks to manage

| Risk | Why | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| ASA complaint about "factory direct" | Misleading-by-implication is a known enforcement area | Use the defensible language ("European factory pricing + local installation"); make breakdown visible on request |
| Race to the bottom on price | Easy for a competitor to undercut by 5% on Google Ads | Compete on trust + IBG + speed + breadth of tiers, not on lowest price |
| Customer perceives 22% as "too much commission" once they see the breakdown | If line-itemed badly, looks like a middleman fee | Frame the £1,650 as "AtoZ service, IBG, 10-yr guarantee" — i.e., what they're getting for it |
| B2B doesn't accept retail tier | Architects/contractors will smell the markup | Pre-built B2B tier with 2–3pp discount and trade portal; never quote them retail |
| Mid-market position squeezed | If a Debesto-equivalent enters UK install game | First-mover, brand, panel relationships, B2B accounts are the moat |

---

## 6. Recommendation

1. **Drop the literal "factory direct" claim** in favour of "European factory pricing + locally insured installation." Same emotional message, no ASA exposure.
2. **Show pricing transparency** as a feature, not a tactic — visible breakdown on request, not buried in T&Cs. This converts "factory direct" from a slogan into a demonstrated fact and is itself a strong differentiator.
3. **Adopt the tiered commission** — 25% / 22% / 18% / 15% / 10–12% across the bands above. Default planning rate is **22%** for the B2C bread-and-butter.
4. **Anchor against Anglian/Everest, not against the Polish factory.** Customers who choose AtoZ are switching from national installers, not from DIY imports. Marketing should frame the saving in those terms ("save vs Anglian", not "save vs factory").
5. **Build the value prop on trust, not price.** Cheaper-than-Anglian is a feature, but the headline is *"the only way to buy a CERTASS-installed, IBG-backed bespoke window from a European factory in one click."*

---

## 7. Bottom line

Customers will buy from AtoZ because the marketplace **collapses three separate pain points into one trusted purchase**: factory-grade product, professional installation, insured guarantee. The 22% commission is what makes that promise deliverable — high enough to fund the service, low enough to deliver a real saving against the national-installer alternative, and small enough that when shown transparently it reads as a fair fee for tangible things.

The "factory direct" instinct is correct, but the literal phrasing is risky. Reframed as **"European factory pricing + locally insured installation"** the same idea becomes legally defensible, honestly substantiated, and arguably more persuasive — because it surfaces the trust differentiator (IBG + CERTASS) rather than implying a price miracle.
