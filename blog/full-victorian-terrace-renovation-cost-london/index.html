Victorian Terrace Renovation Cost in London 2026

Victorian terrace renovation cost in London runs £196k–£450k NET for 90–130 m² on 2VP’s rates — before the period fabric adds its surprises.

By Vladimir Castravet 17 October 2026 12 min read Updated October 2026
Amott Road · Peckham, SE15 · Victorian terrace · architect Nick Elias £140k–£175k whole-house, ex VAT · 14 weeks on site

A Victorian terrace is a building type before it is a budget. Most of London’s were built to one pattern: two storeys, solid 9-inch brick walls, a slate roof, a suspended timber ground floor, chimney breasts on the party walls and a narrow rear outrigger with a side return beside it. This guide prices the typical 90–130 m² terrace on 2VP’s own estimator rate library, shows the arithmetic, then adds what the period fabric costs. For a room-by-room view of any house type, see the full house renovation cost guide.

Average Victorian terrace renovation cost in London 2026

For a period property renovation in London, “full” means every room stripped and refitted, rewired, replumbed, replastered where needed and redecorated, with no extension. The estimator calls it a full property renovation and prices it at £1,900 per m² of net build at Balanced tier. Add 10% contingency and 25% overhead and profit and the ex-VAT price is net build × 1.35.

What affects the cost of a Victorian terrace renovation

The table assumes a sound house. A Victorian terrace is usually a sound house with 130 years of other people’s repairs in it. These items decide where in the range your job lands. Line rates are from 2VP’s rate library, Balanced, net build before contingency and overhead.

Solid walls, lime and breathability

The walls are solid brick, usually about 9 inches thick, with no cavity and no damp-proof course you can rely on. They were built in lime mortar and lime plaster, which let moisture in and out. Cement render, cement repointing and plastic paints trap it. The fix is often subtraction: hack off the cement, repoint and replaster in lime. Lime work is specialist and quoted by the trade, so ask for it as its own line.

Damp that is not rising damp

Most “rising damp” in London terraces is something else: a path built up above the old damp-proof level, a leaking gutter, blocked air bricks or condensation. Injected damp-proofing cures none of those. Read why walls get damp before you pay for a course. Tanking at £75/m² belongs in a cellar — as at Amott Road — not on breathable walls upstairs.

Lath-and-plaster ceilings

Original ceilings are lime plaster on timber laths. Where the plaster has lost its key, it sags and eventually falls. Replacing with plasterboard runs £38/m² plus a £26/m² skim, so 40 m² of ceilings costs 40 × £64 = £2,560. Sound lath ceilings with cornice are worth keeping; the decision is room by room, after a look from above.

Suspended timber floors and air bricks

The ground floor sits on joists over a ventilated void, fed by air bricks in the front and rear walls. Block them — with a raised patio, a render or a rear extension — and the joists rot. Any extension across the back wall has to carry that ventilation through with ducted vents, which belongs on the drawing. Insulate between the joists while the boards are already up.

Chimney breasts

Removing a breast frees floor space but leaves the stack above with nothing under it. It needs support — a steel on padstones, or steel gallows brackets where the engineer and building control accept them — and the work is structural, under Approved Document A. A 178×102 steel is £950 and two padstones £100 in the library, so about £1,050 per breast before making good. Breasts sit on party walls, so cutting one away is notifiable work under section 2 of the Party Wall etc. Act 1996, with at least two months’ notice under section 3. Budget a party wall award at £800–£1,500 with one Agreed Surveyor.

Wiring, lead and cast iron

  • Rewiring. Expect old circuits, no RCD protection and mixed earthing. In the library: full rewire £85/m², so 110 m² = £9,350; consumer unit £480; earthing and bonding £1,200; certificate £480. Total £11,510. New circuits and a replacement consumer unit are notifiable under regulation 12(6A) of the Building Regulations 2010 and Approved Document P.
  • Lead pipes. Many terraces still have a lead supply pipe from the boundary stopcock. Lead is not permitted for new drinking-water pipework under the Water Supply (Water Fittings) Regulations 1999, and the pipe under your front garden is usually yours to replace. Internal hot and cold distribution is a £1,800 lot.
  • Cast-iron soil stacks and clay drains. Cracked stacks and root-filled clay runs are common. A CCTV drain survey before contract turns a guess into a line; drainage runs are a £2,500 lot.

Sash windows

Repair is usually cheaper than replacement and keeps the elevation. Replacement timber windows run £900–£1,300 per m² in the library plus £350 to install: a 1.8 m² sash at the mid rate is 1.8 × £1,100 + £350 = £2,330. Replacing a window is controlled work that has to meet Approved Document L Volume 1 (2021). In a conservation area under an Article 4 Direction, front windows can also need planning permission — see Fulham’s Article 4 position and what a conservation area changes.

Outrigger, side return and front garden

The outrigger is the narrow back wing, often on shallower foundations and the first place movement shows. At the front, the boundary wall and the tiled path are part of the house’s value: a rebuilt brick wall is £380 per linear metre, so a 5 m frontage is £1,900. Cracks above openings usually point to lintels, not plaster — see cracks above windows and doors.

Approved Documents are statutory guidance to the Building Regulations 2010 for England. Your building control body has the final word on your building.

Hidden costs most homeowners miss

Contingency: 10% in the price, 15–20% in your head

The estimator carries 10% contingency by default. That is sized for a defined scope: the risk that measured quantities move. 2VP’s published research puts the contingency for pre-1940 housing at 15–20%, because period houses hide things no survey sees until the plaster is off — rotten joist ends, a failed lintel, a lead pipe under the hall. Both numbers are honest; they cover different risks. On the 110 m² Balanced house, 10% is £20,900 in the contract. Holding 15–20% means keeping a further £10,450–£20,900 aside yourself. Ask that unspent contingency is shown line by line at the end, not absorbed.

VAT: 20%, or 5% if the house has stood empty

Under HMRC VAT Notice 708, renovating or altering a dwelling that has not been lived in for at least two years before the work starts qualifies for the reduced 5% rate on the builder’s work and the materials the builder supplies. You need evidence of the empty period — council tax or utility records, or a letter from the council’s empty property officer — before the first invoice. Consultants billed separately stay at 20%. On £282,150 the difference is £56,430 against £14,108. A house you have been living in does not qualify.

The rest of the list

  • An asbestos survey before strip-out. Textured ceilings, floor tiles and old flues from later refits are the usual places. The Control of Asbestos Regulations 2012 apply before anyone breaks into them.
  • Party wall awards. Chimney breasts, steels into the party wall and loft work all trigger notices, often to both neighbours.
  • Living elsewhere. Fourteen to twenty-two weeks of rent is on no quote.

Victorian terrace renovation cost calculator

None of the pricing above needs a qualification. It needs a tape measure, the house’s floor plan and an hour.

  1. Measure the gross internal area, floor by floor. Ground, first, and any loft or extension you are adding, in square metres. Every rate on this page is per m² of that area.
  2. Split the house into parts. Renovation of existing floors at £1,900/m², loft plus upper-floor renovation at £2,800/m², a single-storey extension at £3,100/m² net build, Balanced.
  3. Choose a tier and check your postcode. Apply ×0.85 or ×1.35 if you are not at Balanced, and ×1.05 if you are in an Inner London postcode.
  4. Multiply by 1.35. That adds 10% contingency and 25% overhead and profit and gives you an ex-VAT contract figure.
  5. List the period items separately. Chimney breasts, ceilings, sashes, drains, the supply pipe, lime work. Each becomes a line, not a hope.
  6. Hold 5–10% more of the net build yourself, then ask every builder to price your list in your order. Anything left out or carried as a provisional sum becomes visible.

When to phase it, and when to leave the original alone

This is the section a sales conversation skips.

  • Doing it all at once is cheaper, if you can afford it. Every phase pays its own set-up again. Site set-up £3,972, rear scaffold £4,500 and two skips £1,000 come to £9,472 net each time, before project management at £500 a week.
  • If you must phase, phase by trade logic, not by room. Roof, rewire, heating and drains first, because they run through every room. Kitchen and infill second. Decoration last. A beautiful front room rewired a year later is decorated twice.
  • Leave sound fabric alone. A well-keyed lath ceiling with its cornice, a sash that only needs new cords and draught-stripping, an encaustic tiled path, a chimney breast you do not need the space from: each is cheaper kept than replaced, and each is part of what a buyer pays for.
  • The case for going further is real too. If wiring, plumbing and roof are all near the end of their life, open the house once.

Frequently asked questions about Victorian terrace renovation cost.

How much does it cost to renovate a Victorian terrace in London?

On 2VP’s own estimator rate library, a full renovation of a 110 m² Victorian terrace with no extension costs £282,150 at Balanced tier, with a range of £253,935–£324,472. A 90 m² house at Minimalist tier is £196,222 and a 130 m² house at Signature tier is £450,158. All figures are NET of VAT and include 10% contingency and 25% overhead and profit. Inner London postcodes add 5%.

How much does a Victorian terrace renovation with a loft and extension cost?

On a 95 m² terrace adding a 25 m² dormer loft and a 25 m² side and rear infill, priced at Balanced tier: ground floor 50 m² × £1,900, first floor plus loft 70 m² × £2,800 and the infill 25 m² × £3,100 give £368,500 net build. With 10% contingency and 25% overhead and profit the contract mid is £497,475 NET, with a range of £447,728–£572,096. Consultants and VAT are in addition.

What contingency should I hold on a Victorian house?

The 2VP estimator carries 10% contingency, which covers the risk on a defined, measured scope. 2VP’s published research puts the contingency for pre-1940 housing at 15–20%, because period houses hide defects that only show once plaster and floors come up. On a £209,000 net build, that means keeping a further £10,450–£20,900 aside yourself on top of the 10% already in the price.

Can I pay 5% VAT on a Victorian renovation?

Only if the house has not been lived in for at least two years before the work starts. HMRC VAT Notice 708 allows the reduced 5% rate on the renovation or alteration of such a dwelling, for the builder’s work and the materials the builder supplies. You need evidence of the empty period before invoicing starts. Consultants billed separately stay at 20%, and a house you are living in pays the standard 20%.

Do I need a party wall notice to remove a chimney breast?

Usually yes. Chimney breasts in a terrace sit on the party wall, and cutting one away is work to a party structure under section 2 of the Party Wall etc. Act 1996, with at least two months’ notice under section 3. The stack above also needs structural support designed by an engineer and approved by building control. Budget £800–£1,500 for an award with one Agreed Surveyor.

Should I repair or replace original sash windows?

Repair first, if the frames are sound. New cords, beads and draught-stripping keep the elevation and usually cost less. Replacement timber windows run £900–£1,300 per m² on 2VP’s rate library plus £350 to install, so a 1.8 m² sash at the mid rate is £2,330 NET. Replacements must meet Approved Document L, and in a conservation area with an Article 4 Direction the front windows can need planning permission.
