A mansion block renovation in London starts with the freeholder's licence for alterations, then works to the lease's rules on hours, floors and noise.

Before a wall moves, read your lease: it says what needs consent and what is forbidden outright. Structural work and moving a bathroom are the usual sticking points. The licence itself usually needs drawings, a method statement, a programme and your contractor's insurance certificates, and you normally pay the landlord's fees.

Then the rules on site. Borough construction hours set the outer limit; many blocks are stricter. The Party Wall etc. Act 1996 applies between flats too, above and below. And the floor matters most to the neighbour downstairs: many leases require carpet or a set acoustic standard, so a hard floor needs a proper floating build-up.
