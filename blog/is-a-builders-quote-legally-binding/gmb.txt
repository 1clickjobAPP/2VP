Yes, a builder's quote is legally binding once you accept it: offer plus acceptance makes a contract. An estimate is only a guide, and the final bill can move away from it.

What protects you is the detail. An itemised quote prices each line with a quantity and rate, names the drawings it is based on, and lists exclusions, provisional sums, the VAT basis and how long the price is valid.

An accepted quote can change only for good reasons: variations you ask for, provisional sums adjusted to the real cost, risks the contract puts on you such as ground conditions, or work the quote excluded. A builder who underpriced the job cannot just add to the bill.

Get every change priced in writing before the work is done. Most quote disputes are really variation disputes.

This is the general position in England, not legal advice.
