Matching bricks on a Victorian house means matching four things: the brick, the mortar, the bond and the pointing. Get one wrong and the join shows from the end of the garden.

Most London Victorian terraces are built in yellow London stock. Victorian bricks were made to imperial sizes and are often taller than today's 65mm brick, so measure ten courses of your old wall before you buy. Grey cement mortar next to old lime mortar is the most common reason a "matched" extension still looks new.

Reclaimed stocks usually match best beside old walls; new handmade stocks are more consistent and easier to order in quantity. Under permitted development, extension materials must be of similar appearance to the house, and conservation areas often ask for samples.

Before the wall goes up, build a sample panel, look at it wet and dry, and sign it off in writing.
