Fire safety in an office fit-out comes down to four things: a reviewed fire risk assessment, escape routes that still work, fire-stopping you can prove, and fire safety information handed over at the end.

If you employ people in the space, the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005 usually makes you the responsible person for your floor. The landlord covers the common parts, and the two of you must cooperate.

New rooms change the fire strategy: longer escape routes, inner rooms, more fire doors, more alarm and emergency lighting. Most fit-outs need building control approval, and the design must meet Approved Document B.

During the works, CDM 2015 puts site fire safety on the principal contractor: hot works permits, alarm isolations by permit, waste out daily.

At handover, ask for the fire safety information Regulation 38 describes: as-built drawings, the fire-stopping record, the fire door schedule and alarm certificates.
