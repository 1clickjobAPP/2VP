Design and build vs traditional tender: the real cost difference is who carries the design risk, not the starting price.

In a traditional tender, your architect completes the design, several builders price it, and you sign with the lowest. But anything missing or wrong in the drawings becomes a variation you pay for, items nobody could price become provisional sums, and you pay a contract administrator to manage it.

In design and build, one company designs and builds. Design gaps are its problem, design and build overlap so the programme is usually shorter, and you deal with one party.

To compare fairly, add up fees, provisional sums, a realistic variations allowance and the cost of time on each route. Then compare final likely cost.
