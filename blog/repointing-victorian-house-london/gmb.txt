Repointing a Victorian house in London? Use lime mortar, not cement: the mortar must be softer and more breathable than the soft London stock brick, so the joints wear and the bricks survive.

Cement pointing traps water behind the brick face. In winter that face freezes and flakes away (spalling), and a spalled brick has to be cut out and replaced.

How to tell your walls need doing: mortar you can scrape out with a key, joints eroded well back from the brick, damp patches on chimney breasts or gables, plants in the joints. Diagonal stepped cracks are a movement problem, not a pointing one.

What drives the cost: scaffolding and access, how hard the old mortar is to remove, the joint finish (tuck pointing is specialist work), brick replacement and any render removal. Ask for each as its own line, and ask for a test panel before the wall is started.

Thinking of removing cement render? Open a small trial patch first, and check conservation area and listed building rules for the front.
