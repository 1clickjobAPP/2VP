Office phone booths vs meeting rooms comes down to headcount: booths for one-person calls, built meeting rooms for meetings of three or more.

Hybrid working means many people come into the office and spend much of the day on video calls. A floor full of four-person rooms ends up with one person in each.

Before you choose, count. For two typical weeks, log calls and pull your room-booking data. Peak one-person calls set the number of booths; peak group meetings set the number of rooms.

If you go for pods, ask these questions before you order:
- Does the pod need its own smoke detector, linked to the building's fire alarm?
- Does its roof block the sprinklers?
- Will someone inside hear the alarm?
- Does it narrow an escape route?
- Can the office ventilation supply the fresh air its fan draws?

Booths usually leave with you at the end of a lease. Built rooms may have to be stripped out. Ask for both priced side by side in writing.
