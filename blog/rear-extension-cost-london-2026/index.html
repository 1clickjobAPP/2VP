Rear Extension Cost in London 2026: Single-Storey, Line by Line

Rear extension cost in London 2026 runs £3,557–£5,650 per m² NET on 2VP’s own rates, so a typical 25 m² Balanced build is £104,625.

By Vladimir Castravet 17 October 2026 10 min read Updated October 2026
Moncrieff · SE15 · loft and extension by 2VP Project cost not published · rates below are 2VP’s library, NET

The single-storey rear extension is the most common way a London house gets bigger, and the most commonly under-budgeted. The build is only part of the bill. On 2VP's own rate library a 25 m² rear extension at the middle finish level is £77,500 of net build, £104,625 once contingency and overhead are added, and about £117,000–£118,000 once the architect, engineer, building control and one party wall surveyor are paid. All NET of VAT. This guide shows how each of those numbers is made, so you can make your own.

The house extension cost guide publishes a band of £78k–£140k excl. VAT for a single-storey rear extension. That band is the typical 20–30 m² London build at Minimalist or Balanced, and the arithmetic holds with two exceptions, stated plainly. A 20 m² Minimalist extension, at £71,145, comes in below it. A 20 m² Signature extension, at £112,995, sits inside it. Everything at Signature from 25 m², and every tier at 40 m², sits above it — 25 m² Signature by £1,244. The estimator also shows each figure as a range of ×0.90 to ×1.15, so the top of a 30 m² Balanced range, £144,382, runs past £140k too.

Building upwards as well? A double-storey extension is a different job, published at £120k–£200k excl. VAT in the same guide. This page is single-storey only.

What affects the cost of a rear extension

Depth: 3 metres or 6

On a house 5 m wide, a 3 m extension is 15 m² and a 6 m one is 30 m². At Balanced that is £62,775 against £125,550 ex VAT. The rule line sits between them. Permitted development allows 3 m on an attached house (4 m detached); 6 m (8 m detached) needs prior approval, where neighbours are consulted. Fixed costs mean the 15 m² job costs more per m² than the estimator's straight rate. See permitted development rules for extensions for the full limits.

Glazing

Glass is the biggest specification lever. A 4 m × 2.4 m rear opening is 9.6 m²: £7,680 in aluminium at our £800 per m² mid rate, £17,280 in steel at £1,800. Same opening, £9,600 apart.

Roof: flat, pitched, rooflights

On 25 m², a warm flat roof in EPDM is 26.25 m² at £190: £4,988. Pitched in slate it is 27.5 m² at £260: £7,150. Rooflights add glass, which counts against the glazing limit below.

Foundations and trial holes

The estimator assumes a 600 mm × 1.0 m trench at £450 per m³. Three new walls on a 5 m × 5 m extension is 15 linear metres: 9 m³, £4,050. If trial holes find clay near a tree and the engineer takes it to 2.0 m, that is 18 m³ and £8,100. Dig trial holes before you fix the price — why foundation depth changes on site explains why.

Party walls, drains and access

Building on the boundary, cutting into a party wall or digging within 3 m of a neighbour's foundations triggers the Party Wall etc. Act 1996: £800–£1,500 per neighbour with one Agreed Surveyor — see the party wall agreement cost guide. Many London terraces have a shared drain running behind them, and Thames Water expects a build-over agreement before you build over or within 3 metres of a public sewer. In inner London, add parking suspensions, a pavement licence at £70 a week and the ×1.05 factor; with no side access, every skip and steel comes through the house.

Approved Documents are statutory guidance to the Building Regulations 2010 for England. Compliance is judged against the Requirements in Schedule 1; the Approved Document shows one way of meeting them. Your building control body has the final word on your building.

The glazing rule catches most people. On 25 m² the allowance is 6.25 m² plus the old back door and window. The 9.6 m² opening above is already over it before a single rooflight, so most glazed London extensions go the calculation route — better walls and roof to pay for more glass. Budget for that calculation at design stage.

Hidden costs most homeowners miss

  • Two neighbours, not one. A mid-terrace extension often needs notices on both sides: up to £3,000 in surveyors.
  • The kitchen product. The rate installs a kitchen at the tier's level. A bespoke one, and the appliances, are extra.
  • What trial holes and the drain survey find. Deeper footings and a moved manhole are the two commonest variations on a London extension.
  • Living without a kitchen. The back of the house is open for much of the programme.
  • VAT. 20% on the contract — £20,925 on the 25 m² example.

How long it takes

The estimator allows 14–20 weeks on site. Before that, prior approval takes up to 42 days and a householder planning application 8 weeks; party wall notices need one to two months; windows run 4–8 weeks lead time and bespoke joinery 8–12. Start the paperwork early and the site start is rarely the bottleneck.

When a rear extension is the wrong spend

Plenty of people who ask for a price do not need one.

  • If the problem is layout, not size. A dark middle room and a galley kitchen are often fixed by taking out a wall and refitting. A new kitchen is £15.7k–£54.9k NET in our kitchen renovation cost guide, against £104,625 for a 25 m² extension.
  • If the side return is the dead space. A side return infill is £55k–£90k standalone and keeps the garden — read the side return extension guide.
  • If you need a bedroom, not a kitchen. A loft adds a bedroom at £2,700 per m² against £4,185 here. Compare them in loft conversion vs extension.
  • If you are moving within three years. You will pay for the disruption and leave before you enjoy it.

Run my numbers →

Or by hand, in five steps:

  1. Measure the footprint. Internal width × depth of the new space. A 5 m × 5 m extension is 25 m².
  2. Pick the tier and the postcode factor. £2,635, £3,100 or £4,185 per m² net build; ×1.05 in inner London.
  3. Multiply, then × 1.35. 25 × £3,100 = £77,500, then £104,625 with contingency and overhead.
  4. Add the fees. Architect 7%, structural engineer £3,000, building control £1,500, and £800–£1,500 per neighbour for party wall.
  5. Add VAT and send your list out. 20% on the contract. Then ask every contractor to price stages A to H in your order, so any gap is visible.

Frequently asked questions about rear extension cost

How much does a rear extension cost in London in 2026?

On 2VP’s own estimator rate library a single-storey rear extension is £3,557 to £5,650 per m² ex VAT in outer London, by finish tier. A typical 25 m² extension is £88,931 at Minimalist, £104,625 at Balanced and £141,244 at Signature. Inner London adds 5%. Fees and VAT are in addition. All figures are NET.

How much more does a 6 m rear extension cost than a 3 m one?

On a house 5 m wide, 3 m deep is 15 m² and 6 m deep is 30 m². At the Balanced tier that is £62,775 against £125,550 ex VAT on 2VP’s rates. The deeper one also needs prior approval rather than straight permitted development on an attached house, and fixed costs make the small one dearer per m².

Do I need planning permission for a single-storey rear extension?

Not always. Under Class A of the GPDO 2015 an attached house can extend 3 m at the rear (4 m detached) as permitted development, or 6 m (8 m detached) through prior approval, within a 4 m height limit and 3 m eaves within 2 m of a boundary. Building regulations approval is needed either way.

Do I need a party wall agreement for a rear extension?

Usually. A new wall on the boundary, cutting into a party wall or digging foundations within 3 m of a neighbour’s and deeper than theirs triggers notice under the Party Wall etc. Act 1996. One Agreed Surveyor costs £800 to £1,500 per neighbour, NET; a mid-terrace house often has two.

How long does a single-storey rear extension take?

2VP’s estimator allows 14 to 20 weeks on site. Before that, prior approval takes up to 42 days or a householder planning application 8 weeks, party wall notices one to two months, and windows 4 to 8 weeks to make.

How much glazing can a rear extension have?

Under Approved Document L Volume 1 (2021), windows, rooflights and doors in an extension should not exceed 25% of its floor area plus the area of any windows or doors the work removes or encloses. More glass is allowed if a calculation shows the extension as a whole performs as well, usually by improving walls and roof.
