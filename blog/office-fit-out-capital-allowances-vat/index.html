Office Fit-Out Capital Allowances and VAT, Explained

Office fit-out capital allowances give fast relief on plant like lighting, heating and cooling, and slower relief on walls and floors, so the split matters.

By Vladimir Castravet 28 November 2026 11 min read Updated November 2026
Amott Road · Peckham · residential reference photo General guide · not tax advice

Office fit-out capital allowances are the tax reliefs a business claims on the parts of a fit-out that count as plant and machinery, or as structures, instead of deducting the whole spend in one go. VAT is a separate question with its own rules. This guide explains, in general terms, how a fit-out is usually split, which allowances applied as they stood when it was written, and how VAT works on the build. It is not tax advice. Take advice from your accountant, before you sign the fit-out contract rather than after, because the way the work is described and invoiced decides how easily your claim can be made. 2VP is a contractor, not a tax adviser, and it benefits when you commission a fit-out. Weigh what follows knowing that.

In 60 seconds — the short answer

  • A fit-out splits into three kinds of spend. Plant and machinery (lighting, power, heating, cooling, ventilation, data cabling, sanitaryware, kitchen equipment, fire alarms). Structures (fixed partitions, doors, ceilings, floors). And, sometimes, revenue repairs.
  • Plant gets the fastest relief. Through the Annual Investment Allowance, full expensing for companies, first-year allowances or writing-down allowances, depending on who you are and what you bought.
  • Many building services are “integral features”. Electrical and lighting systems, heating, cooling and cold water fall in the special rate pool, which gets slower relief unless the Annual Investment Allowance covers them.
  • Structures get slow relief. The Structures and Buildings Allowance spreads the cost evenly over decades.
  • VAT is charged at the standard rate on commercial fit-out and is recoverable if your business is VAT-registered and makes taxable supplies; partly exempt businesses recover only part of it.
  • Itemisation is what your accountant needs. A quote that lumps “M&E” into one line makes the claim harder to support.

Rates and rules below are as they stood when this guide was written (October 2026), from HMRC guidance and the Capital Allowances Act 2001. Governments change them at Budgets. Take advice from your accountant on the rules that apply to your accounting period.

What office fit-out capital allowances actually mean

Tax law does not let a business deduct capital spend from its profits the way it deducts rent or salaries. Capital allowances are the substitute: a set of reliefs, under the Capital Allowances Act 2001, that give back the cost over time, or sometimes at once. In a fit-out, the starting point is to sort every item into the right category.

What is plant and machinery in a fit-out?

Broadly, the things that do a job in your business rather than form the building it happens in. In an office fit-out that usually includes the electrical installation and lighting, heating, cooling and ventilation, hot and cold water, data cabling, fire alarms, sanitaryware, kitchen equipment and loose furniture. The building alterations needed to install that plant, such as builder’s work in connection with the services, can often be claimed alongside it.

What counts as a structure?

The fabric: fixed partitions, doors, ceilings, floors, plastering and most finishes. These do not qualify as plant. The grey areas, such as suspended ceilings and raised floors that exist mainly to carry services, or demountable partitions that you genuinely intend to move, are where your accountant or a specialist adviser earns their fee. Partitions designed and intended to be moved in the course of your business can be treated as plant.

Annual Investment Allowance

The Annual Investment Allowance gives 100% relief in the year of spend on plant and machinery, main rate or special rate, up to an annual cap set by the government. Companies and unincorporated businesses can both use it, and groups and related businesses share one cap. Because it covers special rate items, many businesses use it first against integral features, where the alternative relief is slowest. Your accountant will confirm the current cap.

Full expensing and first-year allowances

Full expensing, available from 1 April 2023 and made permanent in the 2023 Autumn Statement, lets a company deduct 100% of spend on new, unused main-rate plant and machinery in the year it is incurred, with a 50% first-year allowance on special rate items such as integral features. It is for companies only, does not cover second-hand assets, and gives rise to a balancing charge when the asset is sold or scrapped. The 2025 Autumn Budget also announced a 40% first-year allowance on main-rate plant from 1 January 2026, open to unincorporated businesses as well. Take advice from your accountant on which applies to you.

Writing-down allowances

Whatever is not covered by the allowances above goes into a pool and is relieved a percentage a year on the reducing balance. The special rate pool, where integral features sit, is written down at 6% a year. The main pool rate was 18%, cut to 14% from April 2026 under the 2025 Autumn Budget.

Structures and Buildings Allowance

The Structures and Buildings Allowance covers the cost of constructing, renovating or altering non-residential structures under contracts entered into on or after 29 October 2018. It gives 3% a year, straight line, from when the space is brought into use: relief spread over roughly 33 years. To claim it you need an allowance statement recording the qualifying cost and the date the space came into use. For the fixed partitions, ceilings and floors of an office fit-out, this is often the relief that applies.

When it matters in a London fit-out

Capital allowances are claimed in your tax return after the work is done, but almost every decision that shapes the claim is made before the contract is signed.

Can a tenant claim capital allowances on a fit-out?

Generally, yes. A tenant that pays for fixtures as part of its own fit-out, and holds an interest in the property such as a lease, can usually claim on them. What a landlord pays for is the landlord’s claim, not yours. On a Cat A vs Cat B office fit-out, that often means the landlord claims the Cat A base services and you claim your Cat B works.

What about landlord contributions and rent-free periods?

A cash contribution from your landlord towards the fit-out usually reduces the amount you can claim, because you have not borne that cost. A rent-free period is treated differently. Tell your accountant about every incentive before you agree heads of terms; the structure of the deal can change the tax result.

When is the spend incurred?

Usually on the date the obligation to pay becomes unconditional, which on a building contract is normally tied to certified stages or completion. If your fit-out straddles your year end, the payment schedule decides which accounting period each stage falls in. That matters most in a year when rates are changing.

And at the end of the lease?

Removing your fit-out, and the settlement you reach with your landlord, raise their own tax questions, separate from the original claim. The dilapidations office lease guide covers how reinstatement liabilities arise; take advice from your accountant on how they are treated.

VAT on an office fit-out

VAT is charged at the standard rate, 20% according to HMRC, on the refurbishment and fit-out of commercial premises. The reduced rates that apply to some residential conversions do not apply to offices.

Can I reclaim VAT on an office fit-out?

If your business is VAT-registered and makes taxable supplies, you can normally recover the VAT on your fit-out as input tax through your VAT return. If your business is partly exempt, as many in financial services, insurance, some health and education and charities are, you recover only part of it, and the unrecovered VAT becomes a real cost of the project. If you are not VAT-registered, you cannot recover it at all.

Does the construction reverse charge apply?

Usually not to you. The domestic reverse charge for building and construction services, in force since 1 March 2021, applies between VAT-registered businesses in the construction supply chain. It does not apply to supplies to an end user. A tenant commissioning a fit-out for its own use is normally an end user, so the contractor charges VAT in the ordinary way. Confirm your status to the contractor in writing at the start, and check it with your accountant.

What is the Capital Goods Scheme?

Where VAT-bearing spend on building, refurbishing or fitting out a property is above a threshold set by HMRC, the Capital Goods Scheme applies. The VAT you first recovered can then be adjusted over a ten-year period if the proportion of taxable use of the space changes. It matters most to partly exempt businesses and to owner-occupiers. Your accountant will tell you whether your project crosses the threshold.

How does VAT affect cash flow?

You pay VAT on each stage invoice before you recover it on your next return. On a fit-out paid in weekly or monthly stages, that is a timing gap to plan for, not a cost, if you are fully recoverable. Ask for every invoice NET with VAT shown separately, so your finance team can see exactly what is coming back.

What drives the value of your claim

There is no standard percentage of a fit-out that qualifies. The share depends on what you build and how well it is documented. These are the factors your accountant will look at.
