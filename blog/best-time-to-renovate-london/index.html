Best Time to Renovate in London: Plan in November, Build in Spring

The best time to renovate in London is a spring start on site, so design, planning and party wall notices begin in November.

By Vladimir Castravet 1 November 2026 11 min read Updated November 2026
Moncrieff · SE15 · loft and extension Completed · 2VP project

The best time to renovate in London is a spring start on site — and that means starting the paperwork now, in November. A loft, an extension or a structural refurbishment does not begin on the day the builder arrives. It begins with a survey, drawings, a planning route, party wall notices, building regulations, structural calculations and long-lead orders, and each of those has its own clock. Work those clocks back from a March or April start and most of them land between now and the New Year. This guide gives you the timeline, the statutory periods behind it, what each season is good and bad for, and what timing does to your price. 2VP sells build slots, so it has an interest in when you start. Weigh what follows knowing that.

In 60 seconds — the short answer

  • Structural and external work: aim to start in spring. Longer days, less frost, and the roof or back wall is open in the driest part of the year that is still early enough to finish before winter.
  • Internal work: any season. Kitchens, bathrooms and refurbishments inside a weathertight house can run through winter.
  • Plan in November for a spring start. A householder planning application has an 8-week statutory decision period once it is validated. Party wall notices need up to 2 months. Design comes before both.
  • Order long-lead items before you start. Steel, windows, roof glazing, kitchens and joinery are made to order. Ask each supplier for its lead time in writing.
  • Timing changes cost through time, not the calendar. Weather protection, drying, and weeks of site set-up when a start slips are what you pay for. Ask for them as separate lines.

What “the best time to renovate” actually means

It means three dates, not one: the day work starts on site, the day the house is weathertight again, and the day you move back in or get your rooms back. The season matters most for the middle one.

Why does a spring start suit structural work?

Because the riskiest weeks of a loft or an extension are the weeks the house is open: the roof stripped for a dormer, the back wall down for a steel, the foundations dug. Starting in March to May puts those weeks into lengthening days and fewer frosts, and gets the shell closed before the following autumn. Internal trades — plastering, second-fix electrics, kitchen fitting, decorating — then follow inside a dry building.

Is winter a bad time to renovate?

Not for everything. Inside a house that keeps its roof and walls, winter is a perfectly workable season: a kitchen, a bathroom or a rewire does not care about the weather. Winter is harder for work that opens the building. London gets under eight hours of daylight around the December solstice, frost slows concrete and mortar, and a roof that is off in January needs a full temporary cover. None of that stops a winter build; it adds protection, care and time.

What about a summer start?

Summer has the longest days and the driest spells for roofing, rendering and external decoration. Its catch is people, not weather. August holidays thin out suppliers, consultants and council officers, and neighbours who are away are slow to answer party wall notices. A summer start also means your planning and design had to be done in spring — so the paperwork still runs months ahead.

Work back from the date you want the builder on site. The table sets out the steps that have a legal or statutory clock, and the steps where only your designer, engineer or supplier can give you the real figure.

Statutory periods are the decision deadlines in the law, not guarantees. A planning clock only starts once the council has validated the application, and an application can be invalidated for a missing drawing or fee. Councils can also agree longer periods with you in writing.

How long does planning add to a London renovation?

If your project needs a householder application, allow the 8-week determination period plus the time to prepare and validate the application, and add time if the council asks for changes. If it falls under permitted development, you do not need an application, but a Lawful Development Certificate gives you written proof that it is lawful — which a buyer’s solicitor will ask for later. The permitted development rules for London extensions set out which route your project is on. In a conservation area, permitted development rights are narrower and the design takes longer; the conservation area loft conversion guide explains why.

When should I serve party wall notices?

As soon as the drawings show exactly what will happen at the party wall, and at least two months before that work starts. Your neighbour has 14 days to consent; if they do not, a dispute is treated as having arisen and surveyors are appointed to agree an award. That is the step that most often pushes a spring start into summer. The party wall agreement cost guide covers what the surveyors do and what drives their fees.

What does a November-to-spring plan look like?

For a typical rear extension or loft that needs a planning application, using only the statutory periods above. Your designer will set the real dates for your project.

  1. November. Write the brief, commission the measured survey, appoint your designer and engineer, and ask two or three builders for a site visit so you can see who is available for spring.
  2. December. Agree the design. Submit the planning application or the Lawful Development Certificate before the Christmas break.
  3. January. The planning clock runs. Your engineer designs the steel and foundations. Serve party wall notices once the party wall details are fixed.
  4. February. Submit building regulations full plans. Get itemised quotes on the same drawings, compare them line by line, and agree the contract. Your builder orders long-lead items.
  5. March to April. Party wall awards in place, planning decided, steel and windows on order: start on site.

Can I start work before planning permission is granted?

You can do work that does not need permission, such as internal stripping-out, but starting work that needs planning permission before you have it is at your own risk: the council can refuse the application and take enforcement action against what has been built. Party wall notices are also needed before notifiable work begins, whatever the planning position.

Season by season — what each time of year means for your build

Every season works for some jobs. The question is which part of your project lands in which season.

Can a loft conversion be done in winter?

Yes. The critical part is the few weeks the roof is open for a dormer or a hip-to-gable. A scaffold with a full temporary roof keeps the house dry while the new structure goes in, and most of a loft conversion after that is internal. The dormer loft conversion guide sets out the order of works, so you can see which weeks are exposed.

When is the worst time to start an extension?

Late autumn, if the foundations, walls and roof would all run into midwinter. Digging in wet ground, pouring concrete near freezing and leaving a back wall open in January all add protection and time. If your design and permissions are ready in October, it is often better to use the winter for internal work and start the extension in spring.

What drives the cost of timing (no £ figures)

The month itself does not set the price. 2VP does not publish seasonal discounts, and you should be wary of anyone who sells one without explaining where the saving comes from. What timing changes is time, and time is what you pay for in these lines:

  • Preliminaries. Scaffold hire, site welfare, skips, protection and site management run for every week the job is on site. A start that slips, or a build that stalls waiting for a party wall award, keeps them running. Ask for preliminaries as a weekly figure in writing.
  • Weather protection. A temporary roof, sheeted scaffold and frost protection for concrete are real costs on a winter build with the house open. Ask which of them the quote includes.
  • Drying time. Plaster and screed dry more slowly in cold, damp air. Heating and dehumidifiers shorten the wait; skipping them risks finishes failing later.
  • Quote validity. Every quote is valid for a stated period. If planning or party wall delays push your start past it, expect the builder to re-check prices. Ask how long the quote is valid and what happens after.
  • Lead times. A window or steel ordered late does not cost more, but the weeks the site waits for it do.

Frequently asked questions.

What is the best time of year to renovate a house in London?

For structural and external work — a loft, an extension, anything that opens the roof or the back wall — a spring start on site is the usual choice, because the house is open in lengthening days with fewer frosts and the shell is closed before winter. Internal work inside a weathertight house, such as a kitchen or bathroom, can be done in any season.

How far ahead should I plan a spring renovation?

Start in the autumn before, ideally November. Design comes first, then a householder planning application has an 8-week statutory decision period once validated, party wall notices need up to 2 months before work starts, and a building regulations full plans decision can take 5 weeks. Long-lead items such as steel and windows are ordered before the start date.

Is winter a bad time to renovate?

Not for internal work. Winter is harder for work that opens the building: short days, frost that slows concrete and mortar, and the need for a full temporary roof. A winter build is possible with the right protection; it adds care and time, so ask what weather protection the quote includes.

Is it cheaper to renovate in winter?

2VP does not publish seasonal discounts. Price is set by scope, specification and access, and timing changes cost through time: weather protection, drying, and weekly preliminaries such as scaffold and site set-up. Ask for preliminaries as a weekly figure and the quote’s validity period in writing.

When should I serve party wall notices?

As soon as the drawings fix what will happen at the party wall, and at least two months before work to a party wall starts, or one month before excavation near a neighbour’s foundations, under the Party Wall etc. Act 1996. If your neighbour does not consent within 14 days, a dispute is treated as having arisen and surveyors agree an award.

How long does planning permission take in London?

A householder application has an 8-week statutory decision period, which starts when the council validates it. Allow extra time to prepare the application and for any changes the council asks for. Permitted development needs no application, but a Lawful Development Certificate, usually decided within 8 weeks, gives you written proof.
