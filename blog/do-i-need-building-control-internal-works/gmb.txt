Do you need building control for internal works? Yes when the work is structural, adds or moves drainage, changes fire escape or ventilation, or includes notifiable electrics. Decorating, flooring and like-for-like replacements do not.

Common internal jobs that need it: removing a load-bearing wall or chimney breast, a new bathroom or WC on new drainage, a new boiler, a new consumer unit or circuit, and opening up a hallway in a house with a loft room or a second floor.

There are three ways to get sign-off: a full plans application (design approved before you start), a building notice (start two days after notice, inspected as you go) or a competent person scheme (registered electricians, Gas Safe engineers and window installers self-certify).

Keep the completion certificate. A buyer's solicitor will ask for it when you sell. If work was done without approval, a regularisation application to the council is usually possible.

Internal works rarely need planning permission, unless the house is listed.
