Renovation Cost per m² in London 2026: Every Project Type

Renovation cost per m² in London runs from £2,180 to £6,506 NET on 2VP’s own rates, depending on project type, finish tier and postcode.

By Vladimir Castravet 17 October 2026 11 min read Updated October 2026
Alexandra · N22 · whole-house renovation by 2VP Rates on this page · 2VP estimator library, NET of VAT

A per-square-metre rate is the most quoted and least explained number in London building. One builder says “about two grand a metre”, another says four, and both can be right — because one means the net cost of the work and the other means the price you sign, and neither has said which floor they measured. This page puts every project type 2VP builds into one table, at three finish levels, inner and outer London, with both numbers shown. Then it shows you what the number leaves out, so you can turn it into a budget you can defend.

Average renovation cost per m² in London 2026

On 2VP's own estimator rate library, a whole-house renovation at the middle finish level is £1,900 per m² of net build cost, which becomes a £2,565 per m² price once contingency and overhead are added. That is the honest headline for “renovation”. Every other project type sits above it, because every other type builds something as well as refitting it:

  • Flat refurbishment and loft conversion — £2,700 per m² price, Balanced, outer London.
  • New build — £3,240 per m², zero-rated for VAT as a new dwelling.
  • Loft plus full renovation — £3,780 per m².
  • Single-storey rear extension — £4,185 per m².
  • Wrap-around extension — £4,590 per m².

All of those are NET of VAT. Across all seven types, three tiers and both halves of London, the price runs from £2,180 to £6,506 per m².

A declaration before the detail. 2VP builds every project type in this table, and a reference page like this one brings in enquiries. Weigh what follows knowing that — and notice that the section after next is about the ways a per-m² figure will mislead you, including ours.

Two rows you will not find here

Basements. 2VP has not billed a basement dig in London, so it publishes no basement rate. The ground, the water table and the party wall decide that price, and they are site-specific. Our basement conversion guide explains why, and what to price instead.

Listed buildings. Work to a listed house is priced per building, after the survey and the consent strategy, not per m². The fabric you must keep and the methods you may use matter more than the floor area.

Two consistency notes. The loft guide prices lofts by type — rear dormer £75k–£110k NET, for example — and on a small loft that works out above the loft row here, because staircase, roof structure and scaffold are spread over fewer square metres; for a loft, use the loft guide. And the new-build guide publishes a span of £1,800–£3,200 per m² net build; a fully Signature new build, at £3,240, sits just above it.

What a per-m² figure includes — and what it leaves out

Net build or price?

Net build is the cost of labour, materials, plant and site management for the work itself. The price is net build plus a 10% contingency and 25% for overhead and profit — so price = net build × 1.35. When two figures for the same job differ by a third, check whether one is net build and the other a price before you check anything else.

Neither figure includes VAT. Neither includes professional fees: architect, structural engineer, party wall surveyor, building control. Those sit outside the build.

Which floor area?

Multiply by the gross internal area of the space being built or renovated — measured to the inside face of the external walls — not the whole house. A 25 m² rear extension is 25 m², even though it opens into a 120 m² house. A loft is the new loft floor. A whole-house renovation is every floor you are refitting. Get this wrong and a per-m² rate can be out by a factor of four before any other mistake is made.

The same job is “£1,900 a metre” or “£3,313 a metre” depending on where you stop. Neither is wrong. Only the second is the money you need to have.

Why per-m² is a screening number, not a quote

Use a per-m² rate to answer one question: is this project in my range at all? Do not use it to choose a builder or to sign. A rate is an average across jobs. It cannot see your soil, your steelwork, your party walls or how much of the existing house is sound. Two houses of the same area can need very different work — one a refit, one a rebuild behind the plaster — and the rate will price them the same. Once a project passes the screen, it needs an itemised quote against drawings. That is when the real number appears, and it is usually different.

What affects the cost per m²

Why an extension m² costs more than a renovation m²

A rear extension is £3,100 per m² net build at Balanced. A full renovation is £1,900. The £1,200 gap is the building itself. In the estimator's stage split, substructure and structure take 14% of an extension's net build and the envelope — cavity walls, roof, windows — takes 17%. That 31% is £961 per m² spent before a single wall is plastered. A renovation already has its foundations, walls and roof; its structural stage is 12% and it has no envelope stage. A wrap-around costs more again, at £3,400, because it has more wall and glass per m² of floor — the house extension cost guide shows the full bands.

Why per-m² misleads on small rooms

Kitchens and bathrooms are priced per item and per linear metre, not by floor area. The kitchen renovation cost guide prices one 16 m² kitchen at £15,727 to £54,948 NET — £983 to £3,434 per m² for the same room, because a cabinet run costs £250 or £2,200 per linear metre. The bathroom renovation cost guide prices a full strip and re-tile of a 4.08 m² room at £11,387 build: £2,791 per m², set mostly by fittings that cost the same in a big room or a small one. Divide a fixed cost by a small area and the rate looks alarming. Use the item-based guides for these rooms.

Size, tier and postcode

  • Size. The estimator applies a straight rate. Real jobs carry fixed costs — rear scaffold at £4,500 and site set-up at £3,972 in our rate library. That is £565 per m² on a 15 m² job and £212 on a 40 m² one. Small jobs cost more per m² than the table shows.
  • Tier. The tier factor applies across the whole build, not only the finishes. Minimalist to Signature is a 59% rise on every line.
  • Postcode. Inner London adds 5% across the board.

Hidden costs most homeowners miss

These sit outside every per-m² rate, ours included.

  • Professional fees. Architect 7% of the contract ex VAT in our estimator (6–12% is the range). Structural engineer £1,500 for a refurbishment, £2,500 for a loft, £3,000 for a rear or wrap-around extension, £3,500 for a new build. A measured survey at £550, credited back if 2VP builds.
  • Party wall. £800–£1,500 with one Agreed Surveyor, per neighbour, NET — see the party wall agreement cost guide.
  • The right VAT rate. 20% is the default. New dwellings are zero-rated, and 5% applies to some conversions and to renovating a home empty for two years or more (HMRC VAT Notice 708). Do not assume a reduced rate until your accountant or the notice confirms it.
  • Contingency that gets spent. 10% is our default: 5% on a detailed, architect-led scope, 13% on a loose one. Budget it as money you will use.
  • Living elsewhere. A whole-house renovation runs 14–22 weeks on site. Rent, storage and travel are on no builder's quote.
  • What the price does not buy. Appliances, loose furniture and curtains are outside every rate in the table.

Run my numbers →

Or do it by hand. Here is a 75 m² flat refurbishment at Balanced in outer London as the worked example.

  1. Measure the area being worked on. Gross internal area of the rooms you are building or refitting, to the inside face of the external walls. Example: 75 m².
  2. Pick the row and the tier. Flat refurbishment, Balanced: £2,000 per m² net build. Multiply by 1.05 if your postcode is on the inner London list.
  3. Multiply, then add contingency and overhead. 75 × £2,000 = £150,000 net build. × 1.35 = £202,500 ex VAT.
  4. Add the fees. Architect 7% = £14,175. Structural engineer £1,500. Party wall £800–£1,500 per neighbour if the work touches a party structure. Example total: £15,675 before any party wall.
  5. Add VAT and read it as a range. 20% on £202,500 is £40,500. Then read the build as a band, not a point: our estimator shows ×0.90 to ×1.15, which here is £182,250 to £232,875 ex VAT. If the top of that band breaks your budget, change the scope or the tier now, not on site.

Frequently asked questions about renovation cost per m²

What is the average renovation cost per m² in London in 2026?

On 2VP’s own estimator rate library, a whole-house renovation at the Balanced finish level is £1,900 per m² of net build cost and £2,565 per m² as a price including 10% contingency and 25% overhead and profit, in outer London. Inner London adds 5%. Across every project type, tier and postcode the price runs from £2,180 to £6,506 per m². All figures are NET of VAT.

Does a per-m² price include VAT and professional fees?

Not on this page, and usually not anywhere. 2VP’s per-m² price includes the build, contingency and overhead and profit. VAT is added at 20% (0% for new dwellings), and architect, structural engineer, party wall and building control fees sit outside it. On a 90 m² Balanced renovation, the full budget with VAT, fees and contingency is £3,313 per m² against a net build rate of £1,900.

Why does an extension cost more per m² than a renovation?

Because an extension builds the shell as well as the inside. A rear extension is £3,100 per m² net build at Balanced against £1,900 for a renovation. In 2VP’s estimator, foundations and structure take 14% and the envelope 17% of an extension’s net build — about £961 per m² that a renovation, which already has its walls and roof, does not spend.

Which floor area should I multiply a per-m² rate by?

The gross internal area of the space being built or renovated, measured to the inside face of the external walls — not the area of the whole house. A 25 m² extension is 25 m², even if it opens into a 120 m² house. A loft is the new loft floor. A whole-house renovation is every floor you are refitting.

Can I price a kitchen or bathroom per m²?

Not reliably. Kitchens are priced per linear metre of cabinet run and bathrooms per fitting. The same 16 m² kitchen comes out at £983 to £3,434 per m² NET on 2VP’s figures, depending on the cabinetry, and a 4.08 m² bathroom at £2,791 per m². Use an item-based kitchen or bathroom cost guide instead.

Why is there no basement or listed-building rate in the table?

2VP has not billed a basement dig in London, so it publishes no basement rate; the ground, the water table and the party walls set that price after a site investigation. Work to a listed building is priced per building, after the survey and consent strategy, because what must be kept matters more than floor area.
