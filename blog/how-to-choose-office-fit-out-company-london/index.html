How to Choose an Office Fit-Out Company in London

Choose an office fit-out company in London on evidence: an itemised tender, named CDM roles, five insurance certificates and references you can visit.

By Vladimir Castravet 30 November 2026 11 min read Updated November 2026
Moncrieff · SE15 · residential reference photo Office fit-out photo · to follow

Choosing an office fit-out company in London comes down to evidence, not presentations: an itemised tender priced against the same pack as everyone else’s, the CDM roles named in writing, five insurance certificates, a named project manager and references you can call and visit. This guide sets out what to ask before you shortlist, how to compare tenders line by line, which legal roles have to be filled, and how to check references. 2VP is an office fit-out company and wants to be on your shortlist, so it has an interest in this. Weigh what follows knowing that, and apply every test here to 2VP too.

In 60 seconds — the short answer

  • Pick the route first. Design and build, or a construction-only contractor building to your consultants’ design. It changes who you are comparing.
  • Send the same tender pack to everyone. Layout, specification, programme dates, contract form and your landlord’s fit-out rules. Without it, the tenders are not comparable.
  • Ask for itemised tenders. Preliminaries, each trade, mechanical and electrical, provisional sums, exclusions and qualifications all as separate lines.
  • Get the CDM roles named. Who is principal designer and who is principal contractor, appointed in writing.
  • See five insurance certificates. Public liability, employer’s liability, contractors all-risk, professional indemnity and product liability, each in the contracting company’s name and in date.
  • Meet the PM who will run the job, not just the person who wins it, and call references from comparable offices.

What an office fit-out company actually does

An office fit-out company turns a shell, a Cat A floor or a tired office into a working space: partitions, ceilings, floors, mechanical and electrical services, data, kitchens, washrooms, joinery and finishes, delivered to a programme in a building someone else owns. The way it is appointed decides what you are buying.

Should I use design and build for an office fit-out?

It suits most small and mid-sized London offices, because one company owns both the design and the problems that appear on site. The trade-off is control: you agree the design with the company that is also pricing it. If you go that way, write your brief and specification tightly, and ask for the design to be signed off by you before the price is confirmed. Whichever route you choose, someone has to manage it for you; the office fit-out project manager guide sets out what that role covers.

What to ask before you shortlist

Ask these before you send a tender pack. The answers decide who is worth the time of a full tender.

  1. Who will be my project manager, by name? And will that person run the job from start to finish, or hand over after the contract is signed?
  2. Who does the work? Which trades are directly employed and which are subcontracted, and who designs the mechanical and electrical services.
  3. Have you worked in multi-let buildings like mine? With a landlord’s building manager, restricted working hours, shared goods lifts and a licence to alter for an office fit-out to obtain.
  4. What does your programme look like for a job this size? Ask for a sample programme, then compare it against the office fit-out timeline you expect.
  5. How do you price and approve variations? In writing, before the work is done, with the cost and the time effect stated.
  6. How will you report progress? Daily, weekly, with photos? Who receives it?
  7. What accreditations do you hold? Schemes such as CHAS, SafeContractor or Constructionline are worth asking about; your landlord may require one. Ask to see the certificate rather than the logo.
  8. Can I see your filed accounts? Check them yourself at Companies House, alongside the company’s age and directors.

How many fit-out companies should I ask to tender?

Three or four is common practice. Fewer gives you little to compare; more wastes everyone’s time, including yours, and the better-run companies may decline a long list. Shortlist on the answers above, then tender properly.

How to compare office fit-out tenders — and what drives the cost

Compare tenders only once they are priced against the same pack. The lowest total is often not the lowest final account: the difference is usually in what was left out.

What should the tender pack contain?

The layout drawings and specification; the scope split if the floor is Cat A, as set out in the Cat A vs Cat B office fit-out guide; your target start and completion dates; the contract form you intend to use, such as a JCT Minor Works, Intermediate or Design and Build contract; and your landlord’s fit-out guide, with its working hours and approved-installer rules. Ask every tenderer to return the price in the same itemised format.

What drives the price difference between tenders?

Usually five things: what is excluded, how preliminaries are priced, who carries the mechanical and electrical design, how much working is out of hours, and how many items sit in provisional sums. Normalise those before comparing totals. Ask each tenderer to explain any line that is far from the others.

CDM roles, insurances and references

These are the checks that protect you when something goes wrong. They take an afternoon.

Who is the principal contractor under CDM?

Under the Construction (Design and Management) Regulations 2015, a fit-out for a business is a commercial project and you are the client, with duties of your own under regulation 4: making suitable arrangements, allowing enough time and resources, and providing pre-construction information. Where more than one contractor will work on the project, regulation 5 requires you to appoint a principal designer and a principal contractor in writing. If you do not, you carry their duties yourself. On a design and build fit-out, the fit-out company usually takes both roles.

CDM 2015 roles on an office fit-out
RoleMain dutyUsually, on a fit-out
ClientMake arrangements, allow time and resources, provide information, appoint in writingYou, the tenant
Principal designerPlan, manage and co-ordinate health and safety in the pre-construction phaseThe design and build company, or your lead designer
Principal contractorPlan, manage and co-ordinate the construction phase; prepare the construction phase planThe fit-out company
DesignersEliminate or reduce foreseeable risks through the designArchitect, interior designer, M&E engineer
ContractorsPlan, manage and monitor their own workEach trade and specialist

Ask to see the construction phase plan before work starts, as regulation 12 requires, and make sure a health and safety file is handed over at the end. Larger projects must also be notified to the HSE under regulation 6. Separately, the Building Regulations 2010, Part 2A, introduced under the Building Safety Act 2022, place competence duties on the client, principal designer and principal contractor for compliance with the Building Regulations; the client dutyholder under the Building Regulations guide explains them.

What insurance should an office fit-out company have?

Five policies. Ask for the certificate for each, check the insured name matches the company you are contracting with, and check the expiry date falls after your completion date.

  • Public liability — injury to third parties and damage to third-party property, including the landlord’s building and other tenants’ floors.
  • Employer’s liability — injury to the company’s own employees. Required by law for employers under the Employers’ Liability (Compulsory Insurance) Act 1969.
  • Contractors all-risk — the works themselves, and materials on site, against damage until handover.
  • Professional indemnity — errors in design and advice. Essential on design and build, where the fit-out company is your designer.
  • Product liability — injury or damage caused by products the company supplies or installs.

Your lease and licence to alter may set minimum levels of cover for your contractor. Send the landlord’s requirements with the tender pack, so nobody finds out after appointment that their cover falls short.

How do I check fit-out references?

Ask for recent offices of a similar size, in similar buildings, and call the client, not just the contact on the reference sheet. Ask: was it finished on the programme date, how were variations handled, who was the PM, and would they appoint the company again. If you can, visit a finished floor and speak to the building manager, who saw the contractor every day. Check that photos in the proposal are the company’s own work.

In the interest of openness: 2VP’s published case studies are residential, including Moncrieff in SE15, Amott Road in Peckham and Alexandra in N22. Ask any fit-out company, 2VP included, for commercial references you can call.

Frequently asked questions.

How do I choose an office fit-out company?

Choose on evidence. Send three or four shortlisted companies the same tender pack, ask for itemised tenders, and compare preliminaries, exclusions and provisional sums line by line. Then check the CDM roles are named in writing, see certificates for all five insurances, meet the project manager who will run the job and call references from comparable offices.

How many fit-out companies should I ask to tender?

Three or four is common practice. Fewer gives you little to compare; more wastes time on both sides, and well-run companies may decline a long list. Shortlist first on questions about the named PM, experience in multi-let buildings, programme and how variations are priced, then tender properly.

How do I compare fit-out tenders?

Only against the same pack: layout, specification, programme dates, contract form and the landlord's fit-out rules. Then compare line by line: preliminaries, each trade, mechanical and electrical scope and design responsibility, provisional sums, exclusions and qualifications, programme and payment terms. The lowest total often hides the longest list of exclusions.

Who is the principal contractor under CDM?

Under the Construction (Design and Management) Regulations 2015, where more than one contractor works on a project, the client must appoint a principal contractor in writing to plan, manage and co-ordinate the construction phase, and a principal designer for the pre-construction phase. On an office fit-out the fit-out company is usually principal contractor. If you do not appoint, you carry those duties yourself.
