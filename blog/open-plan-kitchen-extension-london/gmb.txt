An open plan kitchen extension in London works when the steel, glazing, heating and acoustics are designed together as one room, before anyone prices it.

Decide them in order. Steel first: the beams that carry the house once the walls come out, sized for the doors beneath them. Then glass: Approved Document L limits glazing in an extension unless a calculation shows the house still performs. Then heating: a room-by-room heat loss calculation tells you if underfloor heating alone will cope in front of all that glass. Then sound: hard floors and glass roofs echo unless you plan soft surfaces and quiet appliances.

One more check if you have a loft conversion: knocking the kitchen through to the hallway can break the protected stair that Approved Document B expects.
